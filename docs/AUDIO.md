# Audio Bibles

Scriptorium ships audio recordings of whole Bibles inside the app: nothing is downloaded,
ever. A recording plays chapter by chapter, marks each verse as it's read (and keeps it in
view), starts at any verse, and carries on into the next chapter.

| Recording | Reads | Reader | Licence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bsb-souer` | Berean Standard Bible (`bsb`) | Bob Souer | CC0 1.0 | [openbible.com/audio/souer](https://openbible.com/audio/souer/) |
| `web-henson` | World English Bible Classic (`webclassic`) | Winfred W. Henson | Public domain | [ebible.org/eng-web/audio](https://ebible.org/eng-web/audio/) |
| `kjv-drama` (sideload builds only) | King James Version (`kjv`) | a full cast | Generated with Fish Audio | [KJV Interlinear, `audio/`](https://github.com/Divhanthelion/KJV-Interlinear) |

Henson's reading follows the WEB Classic ("Yahweh" in the Old Testament), not the 2020
WEB, so it is offered with that edition. He recorded an earlier revision of the WEB,
though: in about 50 chapters a phrase is worded a little differently from the text shown
(2 Corinthians 12:2 reads "I know a man in Christ fourteen years ago … such a one caught up
into the third heaven"). The verse timings are unaffected; his credit says so.

How well the timings fit (October 2026): Bible Hub publishes verse timings for Souer's
reading, made independently. Leaving out each chapter's verse 1 (Bible Hub starts it inside
the chapter announcement), ours differ from theirs by 0.11 s at the median; 90% of verses
are within 0.4 s and 99% within 0.9 s, and 149 of 29,897 differ by more than a second, mostly
in lists of names.

The dramatized KJV is not a recording of people: its voices are generated by AI with Fish
Audio, made for this app (October 2026), about 76 hours. Who speaks each line comes from
SIL's Glyssen character data (MIT), checked against the KJV's own wording and red letter;
each of about 900 speakers has a voice of their own, designed with Fish's Voice Design and
kept for the whole Bible. It is made by the pipeline in KJV Interlinear's `audio/` folder
(`docs/SOP.md` is the procedure, `docs/decisions.md` the reasons):

- **At most 2 voices a request.** Each chapter is spoken in scenes, each one Fish request
  with no more than two voices: with more, Fish loses track of who is who (over the whole
  Bible, lines in another character's voice: 0.4% at 2 voices a request, 3.8% at 3).
  Scenes are cut at narrator lines, and inside a passage where a third voice would join or
  where two voices too alike to tell apart would share a request (those always speak in
  requests of their own).
- **The hearing gate.** A take is kept only if every word of its text is heard in its
  place and every line is in its own voice. A recognizer (Whisper) transcribes the take,
  which is matched with the text word for word; every difference is heard again by a second
  model from a clip around it, and a word counts as heard only where it belongs (the clip
  matches more of the text around it with the word than without it; not somewhere else in
  the clip). A single word not heard, or heard as another word even when the recognizer is
  primed with the text, turns the take down, as do words heard that the text doesn't have.
  Every line, the shortest too, is compared with its character's reference voice (the clip
  the voice was designed from) and with the other voice of its request: a line nearer the
  other voice turns the take down. Fish is then asked again, up to 4 takes, and a scene
  that still fails is halved, at most twice. A person hears what the gate can't settle: a
  word heard in place only when the recognizer is primed, a name said otherwise, a word
  heard as the same other word in two takes (the recognizer's blind spot, or the voice's
  own reading), a line unlike its own voice with no other voice nearer, and a line too
  short (under 0.3 s) or untimed to compare.
- **The checks.** Each chapter is also checked whole: every line's voice against the
  references again (a line in another voice there sends its take back to Fish, and the
  chapter isn't done while one is left), loud noise and babble, and verse starts Fish
  stamped wrongly, which are timed again. Names read inconsistently or wrongly are pinned
  to dictionary pronunciations (CMU, ISBE 1915). A confirmed fault is fixed by rendering
  the chapter again, never by editing audio.

`python -m abible app <Scriptorium>` hands it over: it writes
`data/audio/sideload/kjv-drama.json` from the render's own timings and encodes the chapters
as below, but only once every chapter passes: each through the hearing gate with the
current checks (`abible.redo` has it done, with no fault and no line in another voice
left), each MP3 whole and as long as its timings, its verse labels the KJV's verses of that
chapter, once each and in order ("0" only for a Psalm's title), and its verse starts in
order inside the recording. Verses that leave under 0.15 s a word before the next starts
are listed, not refused. `--partial` lists the chapters not through the gate as warnings
and ships them as they are, for a test build only.

Its terms: Fish's free model was used, and whether its audio may ship in a published app is
still to be confirmed with Fish (KJV Interlinear `audio/docs/decisions.md`, open question 2).
Until then it is a sideload recording (below): store builds leave it out.

## In the app

- **Timings** (`data/audio/<recording>.json`, committed): the reader, licence, credit, and
  each chapter's length and verse starts. `kjv-core` builds them in (`crates/core/build.rs`,
  `src/audio.rs`); the commands `audio_recordings` and `audio_chapter` serve them.
- **Sideload recordings** (`data/audio/sideload/<recording>.json`, committed): not yet
  cleared for the stores. `kjv-core` builds them in only when the build has
  `SCRIPTORIUM_SIDELOAD_AUDIO=1` (`SCRIPTORIUM_SIDELOAD_AUDIO=1 cargo tauri build`; in
  PowerShell, `$env:SCRIPTORIUM_SIDELOAD_AUDIO = "1"` first; the same for the dev server).
  Without it, as in CI and release.yml, the app doesn't know them: no Listen, no Licences
  entry, and the `audio` protocol refuses their files; NOTICE lists `data/audio/` alone. The
  `kjv-core` tests check them either way, and that none is also in `data/audio/`. A build
  packs whatever is in `app/audio/`, so without the switch the app's build (`app/build.rs`)
  fails if a sideload recording's folder is there (`app/audio/kjv-drama/`), or, building for
  Android, in the copy an earlier Android build left in
  `app/gen/android/app/src/main/assets/audio/`: move it out for a store build made by hand
  (release.yml's has neither: neither is in git). When one is cleared: move its catalogue to
  `data/audio/`, run `cargo run -p kjv-import -- notices`, and take it out of `SIDELOAD` in
  KJV Interlinear's `audio/abible/app_export.py`.
- **Recordings** (`app/audio/<recording>/<BOOK>.<CH>.ogg`, not in git: 0.53 GB for the
  BSB, 0.75 GB for the WEB, which is read more slowly, 0.58 GB for the dramatized KJV): Ogg Opus, mono, 16 kbps, speech-tuned. `bundle.resources` ships the folder with the
  app; on Android it lands in the APK's assets. The app serves the files to the page itself,
  with byte ranges for seeking: the `audio` protocol (`app/src/audio.rs`) reads them from the
  resource folder on desktop and iOS, and through the AssetManager on Android, where Google
  Play's install-time asset packs also appear. Only a recording's own chapter files can be
  opened. (Android's WebView cuts a requested range out of the response body itself, so
  there each response starts at the file's first byte.)
- **Player** (`ui/js/listen.js`): the Listen button by the translation, the player bar, the
  verse bar's Listen, follow-along (Settings, Reading), speeds 0.75× to 2×, and the system's
  media controls.
- The dev server serves `app/audio/` at `/audio/`, so the browser preview plays them too.
- A build without the recordings works: Listen is offered, and the first try says the
  recording isn't included and hides it. (Once a recording has played, a chapter that fails
  only says so.)

## How a recording is made

Everything runs from `.cache/audio-tools/` (git-ignored; a Python 3.12 environment made with
`uv`, faster-whisper on the GPU, ffmpeg). Sources go in `.cache/sources/audio/`.

1. **Download**, and record each file's SHA-256 (the BSB's Old Testament zip is Deflate64:
   it is extracted with `zipfile-deflate64`, every CRC checked).
2. **Inventory** (`inventory.py`): every file mapped to its book and chapter; each recording
   must have exactly the 1,189 chapters (the WEB has two byte-identical duplicates, dropped).
3. **Transcribe** (`asr.py`): Whisper medium.en with word timestamps, batched (about 50× real
   time on an RTX 3070).
4. **Align** (`align.py`): each chapter's transcript is matched word for word with the text
   the app shows (from the dev server's API, footnotes left out, line breaks as spaces).
   A verse starts where the voice resumes after the last pause before its first word ends:
   the recogniser's word ends are reliable, its starts after a pause aren't.
5. **Repair** (`gaps.py`, `asr.py --careful`): the batched recogniser sometimes drops a stretch
   of speech; chapters with a long unheard run (and, for the BSB, any verse more than 2 s from
   Bible Hub's published timings) are transcribed again in one careful pass, and the aligner
   uses whichever transcript hears more.
6. **Check** (`check_bh.py`): Bible Hub publishes verse timings for Souer's reading; ours agree
   to a median of about 0.1 s (verse 1 aside: Bible Hub starts it inside the chapter
   announcement).
7. **Export** (`export.py`) the timings to `data/audio/`, and **encode** (`encode.py`) the
   chapters to `app/audio/`, checking each file is as long as its source.
